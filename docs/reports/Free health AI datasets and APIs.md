# Free health AI data: what to acquire first

NHANES, MedlinePlus, USDA FoodData Central, PHQ-9/GAD-7 and openFDA/DailyMed/RxNorm are the free, license-clear sources an AI health assistant can build on today, and the project already holds the hardest-to-replace piece, the NHANES 2017-2018 lab and vitals files. The real gaps are elsewhere. There is no validated symptom-triage source (the 246,945-row `disease_symptoms.csv` is unvalidated, and the notes found no clinically validated, permissively licensed open triage rule set). There is no independent chest X-ray validation set: every large external CXR dataset is gated, and Stanford and PadChest bar commercial use. The RAG corpus, the drug and nutrition lookups, the validated screening instruments with crisis routing, and speech input are all missing. Free hosting is workable for a prototype only, because Hugging Face Gradio and Docker Spaces now require a paid plan and no free tier is suitable for real identifiable health data. For a public app, the dominant risk is licensing rather than data scarcity. Several notes' claims are flagged unverified, and those flags are carried through below. Nothing here is legal advice.

## What the project already holds, and where it is thin

The NHANES files on disk (DEMO, BMX, BPX, CBC, GHB, GLU, HDL, TCHOL, TRIGLY, DR1TOT, DR2TOT, all `_J`) match what the notes call "the best free lab+vitals source", since only NHANES combines a lab panel, vitals and public download ([CDC NHANES](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/default.aspx)). The notes list the 2017-2018 `_J` cycle as also offering ferritin (FERTIN_J), transferrin receptor (TFR_J), folate (FOLATE_J, FOLFMS_J), vitamin D (VID_J) and serum iron/TIBC (FETIB_J) ([CDC lab files 2017-2018](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/search/datapage.aspx?Component=Laboratory&Cycle=2017-2018)). The project's current files do not include these. They are the obvious extension for better anaemia and nutrient labels, and they are small downloads from the same CDC host. Vitamin B12 was not located in any cycle checked (2017-2018, prepandemic, 2021-2023), so treat it as a separate, unresolved task. Whether the existing anaemia, nutrient and other models use these labels is not known from the notes.

Weighting is a correctness issue for anything you report. The notes say to use the weight of the smallest subpopulation containing all your variables: WTMEC2YR for exam variables and WTSAF2YR for the fasting subsample (needed for fasting glucose, triglycerides and LDL), with WTDRD1 and WTDR2D for dietary data ([NHANES weighting tutorial](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/tutorials/weighting.aspx)). If `nhanes_full.csv` was built by merging fasting and non-fasting variables without respecting these, the models inherit that. Whether they do cannot be determined from the notes. Adding other cycles is possible but not free of traps. The prepandemic release (prefix `P_`, weight WTMECPRP) and 2021-2023 (suffix `_L`, adds WTPH2YR) use different weight variables, and NCHS generally advises against combining 2021-2023 with other cycles. The notes therefore recommend training on pooled data but reporting weighted metrics per cycle. File names for DEMO, BMX and BPXO in later cycles were recalled from memory and are unverified. Lab-method comparability across cycles for HbA1c, ferritin and vitamin D was not researched.

Other tabular sets are quick baselines rather than replacements. UCI Heart Disease is CC BY 4.0 with only 303 instances and 13 features ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/45/heart+disease)). The CDC Diabetes Health Indicators set has 253,680 rows but is self-reported survey data with no lab values, and its licence is not stated in the metadata, which is also internally inconsistent about feature count ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/891/cdc+diabetes+health+indicators)). BRFSS 2024 has 457,670 records, again self-reported ([CDC BRFSS](https://cdc.gov/brfss/annual_data/annual_2024.html)). Pima Indians Diabetes was not verified at all (the notes recall 768 rows and 8 features from memory). Gated alternatives are poor population matches or heavy to obtain. MIMIC-IV (364,627 patients) and eICU are ICU cohorts requiring credentialing, CITI training and a signed DUA ([PhysioNet MIMIC-IV](https://physionet.org/content/mimiciv/)). Framingham on BioLINCC requires full or expedited IRB approval and carries commercial-use restrictions ([BioLINCC](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/studies/framcohort/)). UK Biobank charges GBP 500 for three years and its platform was reported closed, with reopening phased from September 2026, which should be rechecked ([UK Biobank fees](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/use-our-data/fees/)). The notes mark the UK Biobank status as a search-summary item with an undated snapshot.

Risk equations are a separate, cheaper gap. Open code exists for AHA PREVENT in Python (`pyprevent`, with a warning that cholesterol must be in mmol/L and the smoking coding is inverted relative to older calculators) ([GitHub](https://github.com/kingrc15/pyprevent)). R packages cover PREVENT and the Pooled Cohort Equations (`preventr`, `PooledCohort`) ([preventr](https://martingmayer.r-universe.dev/preventr/doc/manual.html); [PooledCohort](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/PooledCohort/PooledCohort.pdf)), and an unofficial CRAN QRISK3 package is GPL-3 with ClinRisk asking implementers to confirm parity with qrisk.org ([F1000Research](https://f1000research.com/articles/8-2139)). No code was found for SCORE2, FINDRISC, ATP III or the ADA risk test. The notes also fetched no official guideline URLs for ADA diabetes criteria, metabolic-syndrome definitions or SCORE2, so those thresholds in any rule layer are unsourced until checked. WHO adult anaemia cutoffs (Hb below 120 g/L non-pregnant women, below 130 g/L men) are cited, and the 2024 change only affects children 6-23 months ([WHO via PAHO](https://www.paho.org/en/documents/guideline-haemoglobin-cutoffs-define-anaemia-individuals-and-populations)). WHO ferritin thresholds and reference-range databases were not found. LOINC is free with a free account and accepted terms, but the primary licence page returned HTTP 403, so the redistribution wording is unverified; the notes advise a small in-repo mapping with the required attribution notice rather than bundling the full table.

## Symptom triage and mental-health screening have no validated open data

The notes' central finding for triage is a negative one: no clinically validated, permissively licensed open triage rule set exists. Schmitt-Thompson protocols are proprietary and NHS Pathways is licensed, though both statements are background knowledge flagged unverified. The open GitHub symptom checkers the researchers found are small prototypes with unverified maintenance, for example a GPLv3 checker with no visible clinical validation and an ICD-11-based one under a non-commercial licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/LabinatorSolutions/medical-symptom-checker); [GitHub](https://github.com/prak112/ICD11-SymptomChecker)). Public symptom-disease tables look artificially clean. The 41-disease Kaggle set (about 4,920 records, 132 symptom columns) is reported at 98-100% accuracy in papers, which suggests very clean data, though no source confirmed it is synthetic ([arXiv 2302.12075](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.12075)). Symptom2Disease has 1,200 datapoints across 24 diseases ([arXiv 2502.07755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07755)). The project's 721-disease table is larger than anything the notes found, which is itself a reason for suspicion. Its provenance is unvalidated, and the same pattern applies: a high held-out score on a table like this says little about real-world triage.

For evaluation, the notes point to MedQA (CC-BY-4.0, 11,451 rows) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GBaker/MedQA-USMLE-4-options)), PubMedQA (MIT) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/qiaojin/PubMedQA)) and MedMCQA (about 194k Indian-exam questions, listed Apache-2.0 on an older loading script, so recheck the current card) ([TFDS](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/community_catalog/huggingface/medmcqa?hl=fr)). These measure exam knowledge, not safe triage behaviour. The Semigran et al. vignette study (23 checkers, 45 vignettes, about 57% triage accuracy, risk-averse advice) is the model for a safety test, but the vignette text availability in its appendix was not confirmed ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/node/900623)). The notes' recommended design is a conservative, clinician-reviewed red-flag layer with LLM output constrained to seek-care levels and a custom vignette set scoring over-triage and under-triage separately. That recommendation is the researchers' inference, drawing on public guidance such as NHS or WHO IMCI that they did not verify.

For a knowledge layer, the license-clear options are HPO plus Orphadata (Orphadata described as CC BY 4.0 by a downstream site, to be verified on the source; exact HPO terms not confirmed, since the licence URL returned 404), MedlinePlus Connect (free, 100 requests/min/IP, cache 12-24 hours, credit MedlinePlus.gov) ([MedlinePlus Connect](https://medlineplus.gov/connect/service.html)), and the WHO ICD-11 API (registration required; CC BY-ND 3.0 IGO per the RAG notes, with API quotas not found) ([WHO ICD API](https://icd.who.int/icdapi); [ICD-11 licence](https://icd.who.int/icdapi/docs2/license)). HPO/Orphanet skews to rare disease, so common conditions need MedlinePlus text and ICD codes. SNOMED CT and UMLS are per-user licences with territory conditions, and India's membership status was not confirmed, so the notes recommend avoiding them unless needed ([NLM SNOMED licensing](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/healthit/snomedct/snomed_licensing.html)).

Mental-health screening is the best-covered area, because the instruments themselves are free. Pfizer released PHQ-9 and GAD-7 without copyright restriction in 2010, and phqscreeners.com lists a Hindi translation ([phqscreeners.com](https://www.phqscreeners.com/); [FiercePharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/pfizer-to-offer-free-public-access-to-mental-health-assessment-tools-to-improve-diagnosis)). The public-domain wording came from a 2012 archive, so the live site should be rechecked. A 2021 paper reported item-wording errors in some GAD-7 copies, so copy text only from the official source ([CamCOPS](https://camcops.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tasks/gad7.html)). Hindi PHQ-9 validation exists only in specific clinical populations (leprosy, palliative cancer care, head and neck cancer) with no general-population validation found, so describe it as a translation with limited validation. AUDIT licensing conflicts across sources (public domain per the AUDIT FAQ, commercial restrictions per an NHS listing), EPDS needs copyright acknowledgment, PCL-5 public-domain status rests on vendor sites, and ASRS permission terms were not found. All scoring bands and cutoffs (PHQ-9 bands at 5/10/15/20 with a 10 cutoff; GAD-7 at 5/10/15; AUDIT 8+; EPDS 10 or 13) are flagged unverified background knowledge and must be fetched from official sources before coding. Safe-messaging design matters as much as scoring. PHQ-9 item 9 and EPDS item 10 should trigger the crisis branch regardless of total score (the researchers' inference), with a maintained region table: Tele-MANAS 14416 or 1800-891-4416 in India (KIRAN was merged into it from February 2024) ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/national-tele-mental-health-programme?lgn=en); [BioSpectrum](https://biospectrumindia.com/news/98/24217/govt-merges-kiran-mental-health-rehabilitation-helpline-with-telemanas.html)) and 988 in the US ([988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/faq/)). A JMIR 2026 study warns that safety behaviour seen in consumer chat interfaces does not necessarily carry over to apps built on the API ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e97968)).

Mental-health text datasets should not be used for diagnosis. GoEmotions is Apache-2.0 (about 58k Reddit comments, 27 emotions, crowd-labelled, contains usernames) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/go_emotions)). DAIC-WOZ, SWMH and CLPsych require signed agreements, and DAIC-WOZ has documented interviewer-prompt leakage ([USC ICT](https://dcapswoz.ict.usc.edu/); [arXiv 2404.14463](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.14463)). Labels come from crowd, subreddit or keyword sources, not clinical assessment, so use them only for tone testing or red-team prompts. The project's existing mental-health model should be checked against this: if it was trained on such text, its outputs are community-language signals, not screening results, a point the notes make generally without knowing what the project used.

## Chest X-ray: the owned data is noisy and the external data is gated

The project owns NIH ChestX-ray14 (112,120 frontal images, 30,805 patients, one centre, labels mined from reports by NLP) ([arXiv 1907.12720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.12720)). The licence is not settled: the Hugging Face card says no restrictions but asks for credit and citation, another copy says unknown, an aggregator says MIT, and CC0 was not confirmed. The label quality is a real concern. Oakden-Rayner's visual review of about 700 images found positive predictive values mostly 10-30% lower than documented, plus hidden stratification, and label errors that are consistent between train and test can make test metrics overstate real performance ([AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/artificial-intelligence/article/15624523/study-public-image-datasets-may-have-qc-issues); [arXiv 2010.03975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03975)). Google released radiologist-adjudicated labels for part of ChestX-ray14 (about 37,000 images across two datasets) that give a cleaner evaluation target ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/developing-deep-learning-models-for-chest-x-rays-with-adjudicated-image-labels/)). Whether the official split is patient-level was not confirmed from a source, so check it with the metadata CSV's patient IDs. The existing resnet18 model has no external validation, and per the notes' reasoning it cannot have any from RSNA or ChestX-Det, which derive from NIH images.

Every large external set is gated. The table summarises what the notes verified.

| Dataset | Size | Access | Commercial/public-app position (per notes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CheXpert / CheXpert Plus | 224,316 radiographs; Plus 223,228 pairs | Stanford login/agreement | Research only; Plus agreement bars commercial use and redistribution ([AIMI](https://aimi.stanford.edu/datasets/chexpert-plus)) |
| MIMIC-CXR-JPG v2.1.0 | 377,110 images | PhysioNet credentialing, CITI, DUA | Credentialed licence 1.5.0; commercial terms not read ([PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/mimic-cxr-jpg/2.1.0/)) |
| VinDr-CXR | 18,000 images, radiologist labels | Same PhysioNet route | Same unverified position ([PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/vindr-cxr/1.0.0/)) |
| PadChest | about 160,000 images | Request to BIMCV | Cannot be sold; non-academic use case by case ([BIMCV](https://bimcv.cipf.es/?p=4923)) |
| BRAX | 40,967 images | PhysioNet credentialed | Licence text not retrieved ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9364309)) |
| RSNA Pneumonia | NIH-derived | Public | Attribution-based reuse stated, but NIH-source terms still apply (inference) ([RSNA](https://www.rsna.org/artificial-intelligence/ai-image-challenge/RSNA-Pneumonia-Detection-Challenge-2018)) |
| TBX11K | TB only, train/val released | Public | CC BY 4.0 per project page ([mmcheng.net](https://mmcheng.net/tb/)) |

For external validation the notes recommend VinDr-CXR (radiologist labels, different country), PadChest or BRAX (different continent), and MIMIC's radiologist-labelled test set. The practical sequence for a student or hobby project is therefore to apply for PhysioNet credentialing first, since it unlocks three datasets at once.

Pretrained weights offer a cheap baseline. TorchXRayVision's `densenet121-res224-all` outputs 18 pathologies and can be compared against the project's resnet18 on VinDr-CXR, but its weights were trained on research-restricted datasets and its licence file was not read ([GitHub](https://github.com/mlmed/torchxrayvision)). BiomedCLIP is MIT but its card puts any deployed use, commercial or otherwise, out of scope ([HF](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/BiomedCLIP-PubMedBERT_256-vit_base_patch16_224)). RAD-DINO moved to a Microsoft Research licence in August 2024 ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/rad-dino/discussions/1)). Google's CXR Foundation is open-weight but gated by Health AI Developer Foundations terms that the notes say allow packaging into your own service ([Google](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms)). Whether weights trained on restricted data are legally encumbered was not verified. The notes' recommended practice (patient-level splits, per-class thresholds on validation data, calibration, stratified metrics, Grad-CAM audit for shortcuts) is the researchers' synthesis and is not individually sourced. The shortcut-learning risk is sourced: COVID CXR models relied on dataset-specific markers and positioning and failed at new hospitals ([DeGrave et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.09.13.20193565v2)).

## RAG, drug and nutrition sources: a permissive core exists

For a retrieval corpus, the notes identify MedlinePlus, CDC and the commercially usable subset of PubMed Central as the safest base. MedlinePlus offers a free keyword service (no more than 85 requests/min/IP, credit MedlinePlus.gov) and a daily bulk XML topic set, though its topic-page images are licensed stock and cannot be reproduced ([MedlinePlus web services](https://medlineplus.gov/about/developers/webservices/); [copyright](https://medlineplus.gov/copyright.html)). No source explicitly called the XML public domain, and some text comes from third-party publishers ([FAQ](https://medlineplus.gov/faq/copyrightfaq.html)). CDC content is generally US-government public domain, but that rests on a conference abstract rather than a licence, and no formal API licence text was found ([Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/2012/11/05/centers-for-disease-control-content-syndication)). PMC's Open Access subset has three licence tiers; only CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA and CC BY-ND are commercial-use, and bulk access must use sanctioned channels such as the AWS bucket `s3://pmc-oa-opendata/` ([PMC OA list](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/openftlist); [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ncbi-pmc/)). NCBI also publishes precomputed MedCPT embeddings of all PubMed articles, saving embedding cost ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ncbi/MedCPT-Article-Encoder)). PubMed E-utilities allow 3 requests/s without a key and 10/s with one ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK25497/)).

Several attractive sources are restricted. WHO publications are CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, so commercial use needs permission ([WHO](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright)). StatPearls on Bookshelf is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which also means MedRAG's StatPearls corpus should not be copied into a commercial product ([Bookshelf](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560662/figure/article-32174.image.f1); [MedRAG](https://github.com/Teddy-XiongGZ/MedRAG)). NHS content is OGL but with extra syndication terms whose current text was not retrievable ([NHS terms](https://www.nhs.uk/our-policies/terms-and-conditions/)). The USPSTF API needs a key and requires verbatim reproduction with citation ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/apps/api.jsp)). ADA Standards of Care, Cochrane, Europe PMC and NIH institute pages were not verified. A subtler licence question is that CC BY-ND forbids derivatives, so chunking and summarising ND-licensed text into an LLM pipeline may be a problem. The researchers recommend retrieval for internal grounding with citation-and-link output rather than republishing text, and flag it for legal review.

For drugs, openFDA allows 240 requests/min, with 1,000 requests/day per IP without a key and 120,000/day per key ([openFDA](https://open.fda.gov/apis/authentication/)). DailyMed offers GET-only web services plus bulk SPL downloads and SPL-to-RxNorm mapping files ([DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/app-support-web-services.cfm)). RxNav needs no licence except for its `/proprietary` function, allows 20 requests/s/IP, and its drug-drug interaction API was discontinued on 2 January 2024 ([RxNav terms](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/TermsofService.html)). DrugBank is non-commercial (CC BY-NC 4.0 downloads) ([DrugBank](https://trust.drugbank.com/drugbank-trust-center/terms-of-use)). The open interaction gap is unresolved: DDInter's licence could not be confirmed, SIDER is recalled as CC BY-NC-SA (unverified), and no usable India CDSCO API or dataset was found. US labels also cover US-approved products, so Indian brands need a manual brand-to-generic map and a disclaimer.

For nutrition, USDA FoodData Central is the clean backbone: data are CC0, an API key is required, the default limit is 1,000 requests/hour/IP, and bulk CSV/JSON is available ([FDC API guide](http://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/)). Open Food Facts is ODbL with share-alike, so calling it live with attribution is lower risk than merging it into your own database ([OFF docs](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/)); its India coverage was not verified. IFCT 2017 (528 foods, 151 components) is the right Indian reference but is NIN-copyrighted with no open licence. The community GitHub wrapper is AGPL-3.0, which does not clear the data rights, so ask NIN in writing ([NIN](https://nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/ifct2017/ifct2017.github.io)). Reference intakes were not fetched: ICMR-NIN 2020 RDAs, NIH ODS and WHO/FAO values are all unverified here. This is a notable gap for the project's nutrient model, since the NHANES DR1TOT/DR2TOT files give US intake data but no Indian reference standard.

The suggested RAG stack is the researchers' inference: MedCPT or a general embedder, hybrid BM25 plus a reranker, stored in pgvector. The licences of BGE, e5, PubMedBERT, Chroma, Qdrant, pgvector and RAGAS are all recalled from memory and unverified. MedRAG and its 7,663-question MIRAGE benchmark report up to 18% accuracy gains on six LLMs, which makes it a ready evaluation harness ([MedRAG](https://github.com/Teddy-XiongGZ/MedRAG)).

## Speech input works for English and Hindi; hosting is the weak link

For CPU use, faster-whisper (MIT) is the default: the small model in int8 on an i7-12700K took 1m42s and 1,477 MB RAM on the benchmark clip, against 6m58s for openai/whisper fp32 ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). No CPU numbers were found for large-v3, turbo or distil models, so claims that large models are too slow on free CPU hosts are the researchers' inference. For Indian languages, AI4Bharat's IndicWhisper (MIT) averaged **13.6% WER in Hindi but 28.8% in Telugu**, with Tamil 25.3 and Malayalam 32.3 ([Vistaar](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/vistaar); [arXiv 2305.15386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15386)). IndicConformer 600M (MIT, 22 languages) is a heavier alternative ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)). Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages only, and Vosk's small Telugu model scored 87.9 WER on Fleurs, which is effectively unusable ([Parakeet](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); [Vosk](https://alphacephei.com/vosk/models)). Whisper's own Hindi and Telugu WER was not retrieved. No open Hindi or Telugu medical speech corpus was found; MultiMed covers five other languages and its documented errors include wrong drug names, dosages and left/right confusion ([arXiv 2409.14074](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14074v3)). The notes' conclusion is that any extracted number, unit or drug must be shown back to the user for confirmation. They suggest regex plus unit normalisation and range checks as the base layer, short per-screen prompt-bias lists (Whisper's prompt window is about 224 tokens), and closed-lexicon fuzzy matching ([arXiv 2502.11572](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11572)). Accuracy figures for these correction approaches were not found.

On hosting, the notes verified official pages on 2026-10-31. Supabase Free gives 500 MB Postgres, 50,000 MAU, no automatic backups and pauses after a week of inactivity ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)); pgvector plan availability on Free is unverified. Neon Free gives 1 GB per project with compute suspending after 5 minutes idle ([Neon](https://neon.com/pricing)). MongoDB Atlas M0 is 0.5 GB and pauses after 30 days ([Atlas](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/reference/free-shared-limitations/)). Render's free web service sleeps after 15 minutes and its free Postgres expires after 30 days ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). Fly.io has no free tier ([Fly](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing)). Hugging Face CPU Basic (2 vCPU/16 GB) is attractive for ASR, but creating Gradio or Docker Spaces now requires a paid plan, and only static Spaces are free ([HF Spaces](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview)). Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)). Cloudflare Workers Free (10 ms CPU, 128 MB) cannot run ASR but suits a proxy or static front end ([Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). No free tier checked offers a BAA, and the notes say not to store real identifiable health data on them. India's DPDP rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with full data-fiduciary obligations due 13 May 2027 ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)). Whether HIPAA, GDPR or FTC health-breach rules apply to this app was not researched in primary sources.

## Prioritised shopping list

The order below is the researchers' notes applied to the project's current holdings, ranked by value per effort and by how much a gap undermines safety. Items 1-3 are cheap and unblock the most.

1. **Clinician-reviewed red-flag triage rules plus a vignette test set with expected urgency levels.** Nothing open substitutes for this, and the 721-disease table cannot support triage claims. Seed vignettes from Semigran-style cases once their availability is confirmed.
2. **PHQ-9, GAD-7 (and Hindi PHQ-9) from the official source, with crisis routing.** Free, quick, and mandatory for safety. Fetch scoring cutoffs from official pages, since the notes' values are unverified. Add Tele-MANAS 14416 and 988.
3. **MedlinePlus bulk XML and Connect, plus CDC pages and licence-filtered PMC OA.** This forms the RAG core. Skip StatPearls, WHO and NHS text for a monetised app.
4. **PhysioNet credentialing (CITI course and DUA), then VinDr-CXR and the MIMIC-CXR-JPG radiologist test set for private validation.** Add TorchXRayVision `densenet121-res224-all` as a baseline, and Google's adjudicated ChestX-ray14 labels for cleaner NIH evaluation. Keep these private.
5. **More `_J` NHANES labs: FERTIN_J, TFR_J, VID_J, FETIB_J, FOLATE_J.** Small downloads that improve anaemia and nutrient labels. Treat B12 as unresolved. Carry the correct survey weights.
6. **USDA FoodData Central (free API key) and openFDA/DailyMed/RxNorm.** These give nutrition and drug lookups with permissive terms. Plan a manual Indian brand map and an alternative interaction source, since RxNav interactions are gone.
7. **Risk-equation code: `pyprevent` and a ported PCE, then hand-coded ADA and FINDRISC from the published sources.** Fetch the guideline text first, as the notes cite none.
8. **faster-whisper small/base int8, with IndicWhisper for Hindi.** Add a confirmation step for every number and drug. Do not offer Telugu medical dictation without confirmation, given about 29% WER.
9. **Prototype hosting: Supabase or Neon with synthetic data, and a static front end.** Do not plan on HF Gradio or Docker Spaces being free.
10. **Later or optional:** IFCT permission from NIN, ICMR-NIN RDAs, Open Food Facts live calls, MedQA/PubMedQA/MedMCQA evaluation.

## Licence cautions for a public app

First, treat chest X-ray work as an educational or research demo. CheXpert and PadChest bar commercial use outright, and the PhysioNet credentialed licence is unread for commercial terms and forbids sharing. The notes' safest course is to keep restricted datasets for private internal validation and never redistribute images or derived datasets. Weights trained on those datasets may carry the same restrictions, which the notes flag but did not resolve. Any diagnostic claim would likely trigger medical-device regulation (FDA, CE), which was not researched. Second, the NIH ChestX-ray14 licence is contested and should be checked at the source before shipping a model trained on it. Third, in the RAG layer, avoid non-commercial and no-derivatives sources (WHO NC-SA, StatPearls NC-ND, DrugBank, SIDER) for a monetised app, filter PMC by per-article licence, and keep ND content as link-and-cite rather than republished text. Fourth, attribution and no-endorsement rules apply to MedlinePlus, CDC and FDC; OFF's ODbL share-alike applies to any merged database; and IFCT and AGPL wrappers do not clear NIN's copyright. Fifth, SNOMED CT and UMLS licences are per-user with territory limits, so prefer ICD and MedlinePlus unless India's status is confirmed. Sixth, instruments should be copied only from official sources, and their licence status rechecked live (AUDIT and EPDS in particular). Finally, Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only, free tiers lack BAAs, and DPDP obligations arrive in full on 13 May 2027, so build consent, deletion and breach notification now.

## Conclusion

The notes change the shape of the problem. The project does not lack data in the places where data is easy to find: NHANES is already in hand and the permissive RAG, nutrition and drug sources are free and well documented. What is missing is validation and safety content that no open dataset supplies: triage rules, an independent chest X-ray test set, and clinician-reviewed crisis behaviour. Because the existing models were trained on unvalidated or noisy labels (the symptoms table, NLP-mined NIH labels), the most valuable next step is arguably validating what exists rather than collecting more training data.

Licensing, not availability, will decide what can ship publicly. The sources that would most improve accuracy (CheXpert, MIMIC, PadChest, DrugBank, StatPearls, WHO) are the ones that cannot be used in a monetised product. Many of the notes' own licence conclusions rest on secondary sources or memory, so a short verification pass on NIH, PhysioNet 1.5.0, TorchXRayVision, HPO and the instrument terms should precede any public launch.
